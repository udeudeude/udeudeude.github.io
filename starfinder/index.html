01

Make it yours. Keep the edit link.

First copy and save

  1. Clone the toolkit into a new editable brew. This creates your own working copy with its source and styling. You can also open the public example and choose Source → Clone to New.
  2. Alternatively, download the complete source, then open a blank Homebrewery brew and paste the full file into Brew Editor.
  3. Replace example text with your own mission, stats, and artwork. Give your brew a name and choose Save Now.
  4. Keep the resulting /edit/ address private and bookmarked. It is how you return to your editable copy.

Then edit and share

Once saved, watch the Homebrewery saved status as you edit. Use Share → View to get a read-only /share/ link for players and readers.

Use Get PDF to prepare a print copy. Keep the editable brew and a local backup of its source.

The /edit/ address may grant editing access. Don’t share it as the public project link.

Three ways to use it: view the finished example, clone it into your own editor, or inspect the live source. The downloadable Markdown source remains an independent backup, not an executable installer.

03

Your setting. The same visual language.

Replace the content

Work in Brew Editor. The v7 source includes its own <style> section, so paste the entire downloaded file rather than copying only the text after the stylesheet. Replace the sample mission’s names, objectives, equipment, and character options.

Keep the opening and closing tags of a component when copying it. For instance, a mission card can use this pattern:

<div class="sf-mission sf-wide">
<div class="sf-label">Mission Brief</div>
Your mission text here.
</div>

Add another page

Copy the complete structure of a matching example page and insert a Homebrewery page break on its own line:

\page

Choose the right structure for the material: a dossier for a location, a network diagram for hacking, or a phase-based scene for a starship sequence. Keep essential information legible at print size.

Review each added page in the preview. Text that spills past the printable area may require splitting into a continuation page.

Add images when you have them

Pages 11 and 12 include placement recipes for cinematic banners, maps, cutouts, and illustrated panels. Replace image URLs with direct, publicly reachable images you have rights to use. The source uses art-slot placeholders so the toolkit remains useful without adding artwork.

Keeping a consistent look

The stylesheet establishes a white reading field, navy structure, cyan information bands, magenta/purple accent geometry, and Oxanium/IBM Plex/Rajdhani typography. Reuse those existing styles instead of adding unrelated visual elements to every page.

Restore the toolkit from its Markdown file
  1. Download the .md file above and open it in a text editor.
  2. Select all and copy the contents, including the top <style> block.
  3. Open a new Homebrewery brew and paste the full source into Brew Editor.
  4. Save, rename if needed, bookmark your edit URL, and check each page in the preview.

06

When something looks wrong.

I can view the example, but I can't edit it.

You are probably on the public /share/ page. Clone it when that command is available, or download the complete source and paste it into a new brew. Use and bookmark your own /edit/ link.

“New from File” doesn't seem to work.

Open the downloaded Markdown in a text editor, copy all of it, and paste it into a blank Brew Editor. This is the tested workaround.

The page looks unstyled.

Verify that the full downloaded file was pasted, including the CSS <style> section at the start. This toolkit is self-contained; you shouldn't need a separate stylesheet download.

Text overlaps, gets clipped, or disappears.

Break long material onto a new page, reduce overly long strings, and check for unmatched HTML tags or missing markup. The template demonstrates print-sized components; it cannot automatically make arbitrary amounts of text fit.

My illustration or map isn't visible.

Use a direct URL to an image that opens without logging in. Place your link in one of the recipes on pages 11–12. Check image permissions and consult the relevant Paizo publishing/art terms.

Credits and design references.

This is an independently authored Starfinder Second Edition toolkit intended for use with Homebrewery, which is developed by the NaturalCrit community. Thanks to those creators for making complex page layouts accessible through editable Markdown.

The toolkit studies contemporary Starfinder 2e page conventions: bright reading areas, cyan/navy structure, magenta/purple navigational accents, faceted shapes, and large integrated art. It uses its own page components rather than reproducing the publisher's exact trade dress.

If you intend to publish a Starfinder project, consult the official Paizo Fan Content Policy and Community Use art resources. Other publishing options may require different licenses; the appropriate choice depends on what material you include.

The source includes sample layouts with fictional mechanics. They are not official rules or a substitute for published Starfinder Second Edition material.
