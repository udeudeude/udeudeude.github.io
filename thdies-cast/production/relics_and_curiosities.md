# Relics and Curiosities

## Production brief

Eighty independent, system-neutral encounters centered on portable objects and their uncertain histories. Each entry presents a tangible object, an observable anomaly, and an opening for player action. Do not prescribe explanation, reward, or combat. Spoken narration omits entry numbers and headings. Separate encounters with a brief natural pause, without electronic cues or music. On seeking, disregard the encounter already in progress and use the next one heard from its beginning. No fixed runtime is claimed before audio rendering.

**Brother Rubicon opening observation:** Objects remember more than their owners. They are less inclined to speak of it.

**Fixed identification:** I am Brother Rubicon. The die's cast.

**Performance:** A subtle 0.3-second beat before and after the fixed identification, with no break inside it.

**Closing coda, not an encounter:** There ought to be an inventory. I have never found one that was complete.

## Encounters

### 01 | The Key with No Teeth
A locksmith offers a heavy iron key with a perfectly smooth blade. He says it opens only doors that have been locked from the inside. His apprentice claims it has opened three doors this week, none of which had keyholes.

### 02 | The Unspent Coin
A silver coin returns to its owner's pocket whenever it is spent. Its portrait changes each time. The merchant who offers it for sale insists that the coin is not cursed, only particular about its purchases.

### 03 | The Borrowed Compass
A brass compass points steadily toward whoever last told its bearer a lie. When placed on a table between the travelers, its needle circles once and then settles on an empty chair.

### 04 | The Needle and the Sea
A bone sewing needle is threaded with a length of blue silk that is always wet. A seamstress says it can mend anything torn by water. She refuses to explain why the thread grows shorter whenever the tide rises.

### 05 | The Jar of Thursday
A sealed clay jar bears a label reading 'Thursday, before supper.' From inside comes the smell of onions and the sound of a family arguing quietly. Its owner is trying to exchange it for a Friday.

### 06 | The Mirror for Guests
A small hand mirror shows every person in a room except its holder. Behind the absent reflection stands an unfamiliar guest, who appears to be listening closely. The guest's clothes change whenever the mirror is lowered.

### 07 | The Last Button
A tailor displays a single ivory button from a coat said to have belonged to a king. Whenever someone fastens it to clothing, every other button comes undone. The tailor considers this a mark of authenticity.

### 08 | The Bell of Small Things
A thumb-sized bell rings whenever something nearby is lost. It is silent when coins or jewelry go missing, but rings furiously when a child forgets a song. The seller wants to know why it has begun ringing in its box.

### 09 | The Map of One Room
A folded map depicts a single ordinary room in exquisite detail. Its doors, furniture, and shadows change whenever it is unfolded. The cartographer says the room is somewhere nearby, but has never succeeded in entering it.

### 10 | The Candle's Shadow
A candle casts a shadow even when unlit. When lit, the shadow remains but begins to move independently, pointing at objects that have been hidden or deliberately overlooked. The wick never seems to diminish.

### 11 | The Spoon of Remembrance
An old pewter spoon makes every meal taste of something the diner once ate but can no longer remember. A traveler has carried it for forty years, hoping to taste a particular soup. Today the spoon tastes of ashes.

### 12 | The Pairless Glove
A finely embroidered left glove fits any hand. Its wearer can feel someone gently holding the other hand, although nothing is visible. A faded name is stitched inside, and its last letter changes each morning.

### 13 | The Unfinished Letter
A letter written in a beautiful hand ends midway through a sentence. Each person who reads it sees a different final word, although the ink does not move. Its envelope is addressed to the next person who opens it.

### 14 | The Hourglass of Hesitation
An hourglass contains only a few grains of black sand. They fall whenever its owner is about to make a decision and stop when the decision is made. A shopkeeper is trying to sell it before the last grain falls.

### 15 | The Crown for One Day
A circlet of woven copper wire makes its wearer recognizable to strangers as someone they have been expecting. None agree on who the wearer is. By sunset, each stranger claims to have known all along that it was a mistake.

### 16 | The Folded Door
A small wooden door, complete with hinges and latch, has been folded flat like paper. When unfolded and leaned against a wall, it opens into a dim passage. Its maker warns that the passage is longer on the return journey.

### 17 | The Weather Stone
A smooth stone is warm before snow, cold before heat, and damp before drought. The farmer who found it says the predictions have never been wrong. Today it is beating faintly, like a second heart.

### 18 | The Photograph Before the Camera
A sepia portrait shows the travelers gathered around an object they have not yet found. The portrait is older than any of them. On its reverse, a careful hand has written 'Please return the original.'

### 19 | The Thimble of Names
A brass thimble bears dozens of names too small to read without a lens. When worn, one name becomes legible and its bearer hears someone nearby answer to it. The newest name has not yet been engraved.

### 20 | The Empty Reliquary
A glass reliquary contains nothing visible, but its label gives the exact weight of what it holds. Every scale confirms the weight. Its custodian says the contents are still there, though the world has forgotten how to see them.

### 21 | The Chalk That Remembers
A piece of chalk writes only words its holder has spoken before. A schoolmaster has used it for years to settle arguments. Yesterday it wrote a sentence in a language he cannot speak.

### 22 | The Fisherman's Knot
A length of rope has one knot that cannot be untied or cut. When thrown into water, it draws itself toward whatever lies deepest beneath the surface. A fisherman says it has begun pulling toward the sky.

### 23 | The Coat of Many Winters
A threadbare coat always sheds snow when taken from its peg, even in midsummer. Each time it is worn, different initials appear stitched inside. Today's initials belong to a living child.

### 24 | A Pocketful of Dawn
A leather purse contains a faint golden light that fades at sunrise and brightens after sunset. Its clasp opens only in darkness. A traveler offers it for a night without dreams.

### 25 | The Dancer's Shoes
A pair of worn slippers taps whenever music is played nearby. On the floor together, they trace the steps of an unfamiliar dance. A visiting musician recognizes the dance as a funeral rite.

### 26 | The Unbroken Cup
A cracked porcelain cup spills nothing, however carelessly carried. Yet a drop of water remains at the bottom after it has been emptied. A potter swears the cup's former owner never drank water.

### 27 | A Lantern for the Lost
An oil lantern shines only toward people who believe they are lost. Its flame points through walls and down cellars. Tonight, although everyone knows the road home, the flame points toward its own bearer.

### 28 | The Book of Margins
An ordinary book has blank pages but crowded marginal notes criticizing an argument that cannot be found. A scholar claims the missing text is the finest treatise she ever read. She will not say how she knows.

### 29 | The Saltbox
A wooden saltbox refills itself each night, even when emptied. Its salt tastes faintly of the sea, and grains arrange themselves into coastlines. This morning the coastline was moving.

### 30 | The Innkeeper's Candleholder
An iron candleholder has space for five candles, but only four can be lit at once. Lighting the fifth extinguishes a candle somewhere else. An innkeeper keeps it beneath the counter for emergencies.

### 31 | The Seamstress's Scissors
A pair of scissors cuts thread but refuses cloth. The seamstress who owned them claimed they could sever a promise. When opened near two people who have quarreled, the blades tremble and shut.

### 32 | The Librarian's Bookmark
A ribbon bookmark always lies between the pages most needed by whoever opens the book. A librarian tried it in a dictionary and found a word erased from every other edition.

### 33 | The Stone with a Doorbell
A river stone bears a brass pushbutton. Pressing it produces a distant doorbell followed by footsteps. Nobody has answered yet, though a patient collector presses once each morning.

### 34 | The Widow's Dice
Two ivory dice have no markings, yet their owner insists every throw has a definite result. Onlookers sometimes agree on a number before the dice are picked up. They never agree afterward.

### 35 | The Glass Bird
A tiny glass bird can rest on any windowsill. At dawn it sings the call of a species no naturalist recognizes. One listener claims to have heard the same song deep underground.

### 36 | The Folding Ruler
A carpenter's ruler measures every object correctly but gives different lengths to the same road each day. It grows warm when the road is closed. The carpenter has started carrying it to funerals.

### 37 | The Tin Soldier's Return
A dented tin soldier always faces the nearest battlefield, even inside a sealed box. Its current direction points to a meadow where children are preparing a festival.

### 38 | The Ring of Recognition
A copper ring feels heavier whenever its wearer meets someone known before. At a crowded feast it becomes almost impossible to lift the hand, although the wearer remembers no familiar faces.

### 39 | The Brass Ear
A small brass ear, removed from an unknown statue, repeats the last sound heard in an empty room. It refuses human speech when anyone listens. A watchman hears his footsteps from the night before.

### 40 | The Needle of the North
A silver hairpin bends toward whichever road its owner has most often avoided. A courier has carried it for years and now finds it pointing directly into her house.

### 41 | The Broken Seal
A wax seal stamped with an unfamiliar crest cannot be softened by heat. Letters closed with it arrive without anyone remembering delivering them. One has returned addressed to its sender's childhood name.

### 42 | The Rainmaker's Umbrella
A black umbrella remains dry inside the fiercest storm. Opened indoors, it causes rain elsewhere in the building. Its owner has marked seventeen names on the handle, each carefully crossed out.

### 43 | The Clock Without Hands
A pocket watch ticks steadily but has no hands. Held to the ear it lets the bearer hear something happening one minute away. A jeweler says it ticks backward when nobody holds it.

### 44 | A Locket of Smoke
A silver locket opens to release fragrant smoke. The smoke gathers into the outline of a room before vanishing. Its owner carries a list of houses where the room has not been found.

### 45 | The Pebble Choir
Seven river pebbles arranged in a circle hum different notes when covered by a hand. One has been silent since a bridge collapsed. A musician wants the set taken across the river.

### 46 | The Red Thread
A spool of red thread unwinds only when its free end is tied to something belonging to a stranger. Once tied, it leads toward the stranger's most cherished possession, not always where expected.

### 47 | The Candle That Casts No Light
A pale candle burns without illuminating anything. Instead, nearby shadows become sharply defined and oddly detailed. The shadows show small objects that have not been placed in the room.

### 48 | The Penitent's Comb
An old wooden comb catches on knots that are not there. A barber says each snag marks a forgotten promise. A traveling monk asks him to cut the comb in half, but every saw blade slips.

### 49 | The Copper Birdcage
An empty birdcage makes the sound of wings when carried through certain streets. Feathers appear on its floor each winter. A collector insists the bird remains inside, though not at the same hour.

### 50 | The Cobbler's Last
A wooden shoemaker's last changes size whenever someone puts it beneath a bed. Each morning it fits an absent person's foot. The cobbler is trying to learn whose feet it measured first.

### 51 | The Orchard Knife
A folding knife peels fruit without touching its skin. Every peel falls in one long spiral. Yesterday the spiral formed a clear letter, and today the knife refuses to open.

### 52 | The Violet Ink
Purple ink dries instantly on parchment but never on skin. Words written on a hand fade only after the writer has kept them true. A clerk has carried the same sentence for twenty years.

### 53 | A Bone Flute
A bone flute has one hole too many. Played normally it makes no sound, but nearby dogs lift their heads and follow an invisible melody. The maker scratched a warning from its case.

### 54 | The Last Page
A loose page has fallen from a book nobody identifies. Its writing is legible only at twilight. Every evening the ending changes, but its first sentence is always an apology.

### 55 | The Winter Apple
An apple carved from red stone smells of ripe fruit. Set beside real apples, it slowly changes their color. A gardener has kept it for decades and insists it will one day be ready to eat.

### 56 | The Lantern's Twin
Two matching lanterns light whenever either is lit, although kept in distant villages. A courier carried messages between their owners. Yesterday one lantern began flashing with no flame inside.

### 57 | The Weight of a Feather
A white feather balances exactly against any object placed on the opposite side of a scale. A merchant used it for valuables. A child placed a portrait on the scale, and the feather fell through the pan.

### 58 | The Mason's Plumb Line
A plumb bob points toward the earth's center everywhere except one valley. There, it hangs toward an abandoned orchard. A mason says the deviation has been growing for years.

### 59 | The Second Portrait
An oval miniature shows whoever last looked at it dressed as a stranger from another century. The face changes only when covered. A noblewoman recognizes the clothing and refuses to look.

### 60 | The Traveling Teacup
A blue teacup appears among the belongings of whoever most recently invited a stranger to dinner. Its chip never changes. Its previous owner insists it crossed oceans without leaving her kitchen.

### 61 | The Clockmaker's Tooth
An ivory gear tooth rolls uphill on a table. Every workshop record says its clock never existed. A clockmaker has built a mechanism around the tooth but dares not wind it.

### 62 | The Harp's Missing String
A harp has six strings and seven tuning pegs. Plucking the empty peg produces a note no listener can describe afterward. A minstrel will give it away if the recipient promises never to tune it.

### 63 | The Wax Tablet
A child's wax tablet preserves footprints instead of letters. Each new print is smaller than the last. The trail leads toward a river nobody has crossed this season.

### 64 | The Locksmith's Measuring Tape
A measuring tape gives ordinary dimensions for everything except locks. Each lock appears an inch deeper than its door. A locksmith has started finding tiny doorways in the measured distances.

### 65 | The Coin with Two Heads
A coin bears different portraits on both sides. When tossed, neither portrait appears faceup; each looks toward the edge. A moneychanger calls it an excellent omen but refuses to accept it.

### 66 | The Cricket's Box
A lacquered box chirps once each evening, though it is empty. The sound comes earlier whenever the box is moved closer to a particular hill. The boxmaker has never visited the hill.

### 67 | The Sleeper's Hourglass
An hourglass of blue sand empties only while its owner sleeps. It refills upon waking. The grains are nearly gone, and a doctor has advised the owner not to sleep tonight.

### 68 | The Weaver's Shuttle
A wooden shuttle carries thread of an unfamiliar color. It slides through empty air as though between invisible strands. By morning, a patch of the room has become difficult to see through.

### 69 | The Mapmaker's Eraser
A pink eraser removes ink from maps but also removes the corresponding place from everybody's directions. A cartographer erased a minor lane. The lane remains, yet no one remembers how to reach it.

### 70 | The Bellmaker's Tongue
A bronze bell without a clapper sounds whenever anyone lies nearby. A magistrate paid dearly for it. The bell is silent in court but rings constantly at the magistrate's home.

### 71 | The Cabinet of One Drawer
A little wooden drawer opens on any flat surface. It always contains the same gray stone. The stone's weight changes according to how long the drawer has remained closed.

### 72 | The Seamless Sack
A canvas bag has no stitching and no visible opening. Objects pass through its cloth but cannot be retrieved twice. One object occasionally knocks from within.

### 73 | The Brass Sparrow's Foot
A mechanical bird's brass foot grips whatever is most likely to fall next. It has held a cup, a book, and a loose roof tile. Today it clings to a person's sleeve.

### 74 | The Musician's Mute
A brass mute silences any instrument into which it is fitted. The musician still hears the tune through the floorboards. Others hear nothing, but sometimes begin tapping in time.

### 75 | The Pearl of Yesterday
A pearl grows dull whenever somebody speaks of yesterday. It shines again if the speaker corrects a memory. A historian uses it during interviews and grows less certain of everything.

### 76 | The Flint That Burns Blue
Flint strikes blue sparks even against soft wood. They never ignite anything but briefly illuminate writing hidden beneath paint. A painter asks that it not be used in her studio.

### 77 | The Glazier's Shard
A triangular shard of glass shows only sky, regardless of what lies beyond it. Held against a wall, it reveals clouds. One afternoon birds crossed the shard though the sky outside was empty.

### 78 | The Shepherd's Whistle
A whistle can be heard only by animals belonging to nobody. A shepherd once used it to gather strays. Now creatures arrive that no one in the village has ever seen.

### 79 | The Paper Crown
A crown folded from paper remains dry in rain and does not burn. Everyone who wears it feels obliged to announce a new law. Most remove it before speaking.

### 80 | The Needle's Eye
A needle's eye reveals a wide country road when looked through, no matter which way it is held. In the distance someone walks toward the observer. A seamstress refuses to thread it while the traveler is visible.
